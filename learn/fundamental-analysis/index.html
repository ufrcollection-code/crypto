Fundamental analysis: what is underneath the price?

Fundamental analysis asks what may drive an asset’s value or demand over time. The evidence differs by market: a company has revenue and cash flow, a blockchain has network and token mechanics, while a currency is influenced by rates, inflation and economic activity. The method is not about finding one perfect number; it is about making assumptions visible.

The same framework, different evidence

For a stock, examine revenue quality, margins, balance-sheet strength, free cash flow, competition and valuation expectations. For crypto, examine protocol use, token supply, unlocks, governance, security and the relationship between activity and token demand. For forex, examine interest-rate expectations, inflation, employment, trade and political risk.

These indicators do not point in a single mechanical direction. Strong growth can be priced in already. A low valuation can reflect serious problems. The analysis becomes useful when you explain how the evidence connects to a future scenario.

  • Driver: what could change demand or cash generation?
  • Mechanism: why would the change affect the asset?
  • Expectation: what is already reflected in price?
  • Risk: what evidence would break the thesis?

Valuation is a range of assumptions

A valuation model is a structured way to express assumptions about future cash flows, growth, margins, discount rates or supply. Small changes in assumptions can create large changes in the result. Treat the output as a scenario range, not as an objective target handed down by a spreadsheet.

For learners, the most important habit is sensitivity analysis. Ask what happens if growth is lower, costs are higher, rates change or dilution increases. A thesis that only works under one optimistic case is fragile.

Create an evidence trail

Record the source and date for every important claim. Prefer company filings, official protocol documentation, central-bank releases and reputable data providers over anonymous posts. If two sources disagree, note the disagreement instead of hiding it.

Set a review trigger: a result, policy change, supply unlock or security incident that would make you revisit the thesis. That turns research into a living document and reduces attachment to an old story.

Worked example

A balanced thesis

‘The company has growing recurring revenue and net cash, but the valuation assumes margin expansion. I would revisit the thesis if customer retention falls or cash conversion weakens for several quarters.’ The strength is in the conditions, not the confidence of the wording.

Quick review

Carry these four ideas forward.

  • Use primary sources where possible.
  • Write the mechanism, not just the metric.
  • Stress-test optimistic assumptions.
  • Record what would change your mind.

Common questions

Before you move on

Does fundamental analysis work for crypto?+

It can be adapted to protocol use, token economics, security and governance, but the evidence is different from company accounting and can be less mature.

What is the most important stock metric?+

There is no universal metric. The right measure depends on the business model, capital intensity, growth, leverage and the question you are asking.

Can fundamentals predict a short-term price?+

Fundamentals often work on longer horizons and can remain disconnected from price for long periods. Short-term prices are also affected by flows, positioning and news.
