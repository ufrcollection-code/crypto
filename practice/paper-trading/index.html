What this practice tool can teach

A simulator gives you a place to test the mechanics of a plan: choose an instrument, decide a side, enter a quantity, record the fill and close the paper position. It cannot recreate every live condition such as slippage, liquidity, emotional pressure or platform failure.

Treat each order as a journal entry. Write the setup and invalidation before clicking, then review whether you followed your process. A profitable simulated trade can still be a poor decision if it ignored the rule.

  • Order entry and exit
  • Exposure and unrealised result
  • Basic long and short practice
  • A repeatable review habit

The sample market is deliberately limited

The instrument list uses clearly labelled example prices for a few crypto, forex and stock symbols. There is no broker connection, no wallet, no deposit flow and no claim that the displayed price is current. Use another trusted source when you need real data for research.

Resetting the simulator clears the local sample portfolio. It does not affect a real account because none is connected.

!

Paper trading is practice, not evidence that a strategy will make money with real capital.
