What practice can prove

Test behaviour,
not future profit.

Paper trading can show whether you understand order direction, quantity, exposure and closing mechanics. It can also reveal whether you record a reason before acting and follow the invalidation you planned.

It cannot recreate live liquidity, slippage, funding, emotional pressure or every platform rule. Treat a simulated result as process evidence—not proof that a strategy will produce the same outcome with real money.

For a useful first session, choose one sample instrument and write an entry reason, invalidation level and maximum cash risk before opening a position. During later sessions, change only one variable at a time. Review whether your decision followed the written rule, not whether the example happened to show a gain.

After a small sample, group decisions by setup and note repeated mistakes such as oversizing, late entries or moving the planned exit. Connect the simulator with the position-size calculator and risk-reward calculator so each paper order begins with visible assumptions.
